About RoomGuard

RoomGuard is a web system that classifies room occupancy into two classes — Occupied and Empty — from ambient sensor signals on a smartphone: light intensity (Lux) and sound level (dB).

Why it matters

Knowing whether a room is actually in use helps save energy: lights and air conditioning left running in an empty room are wasted. A phone placed in the room is a cheap sensor — no dedicated hardware is needed.

Data

  • Public (75%, 1,500 rows): UCI Room Occupancy Estimation dataset (dataset 864), archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/864.
  • Private (25%, 500 rows): light and microphone recordings from group members' smartphones using the Phyphox app, placed inside the room with a 30-second logging interval and manual labels.
  • Balance: 1,000 Occupied / 1,000 Empty. Split 70/15/15 (train 1,400 / validation 300 / test 300).

Method

A Decision Tree (gini, max_depth=5, min_samples_leaf=10) was trained on the two features Light_Lux and Sound_dB. It reached 97.29% cross-validation accuracy and 95.33% accuracy on the held-out test set. Sound level dominates the decision (90.06% feature importance); light contributes the remaining 9.94%.

Limitations

The model can be fooled when the room is quiet but occupied (for example, someone reading silently in dim light) or noisy but empty (for example, sound leaking in from a corridor). Labels in the private set were assigned manually, so they carry some subjectivity.
