RoomGuard

Deciding whether a room is occupied or empty, from two simple signals on a phone: light intensity (Lux) and sound level (dB). The model is a Decision Tree, trained on 2,000 rows of data.

What matters most

Sound 90.06% · Light 9.94%. The first rule is simple: if the sound is above 46.35 dB, the model decides the room is occupied right away. If the sound is below 41.5 dB, light is what decides.

The data is split into 70% training (1,400), 15% validation (300), and 15% testing (300). Labels for the private data were written from a manual observation sheet every 30 seconds — whether someone was there or not — not guessed from the sensor values. Public data: archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/864.

Model: Decision Tree (gini, max depth 5, min samples per leaf 10). 5-fold cross-validation accuracy: 97.29%. One thing worth admitting: on the private test data the accuracy is 90.14%, lower than the public 96.94% — phone data is simply noisier and covers more varied situations.
